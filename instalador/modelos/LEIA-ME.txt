AMDLSS5 MOD {VERSAO}

Run AMDLSS5-Instalador.exe: it finds the games installed on this PC (Steam, Epic, GOG, Ubisoft, EA, Battle.net, Xbox),
shows each one with its cover and status, and installs, updates or removes the mod with one click.
To remove: open the installer, pick the game and click Remove (everything goes back as it was).
A game that is not listed can be added with "Add game manually...".
In game: use FSR 3.1 (Shadow of the Tomb Raider: use XeSS; Red Dead Redemption 2: run the game in DirectX 12 and
choose AMD FSR 2). The neural network starts ON: F10 turns it off and on. End opens the mod menu.
The package includes dlssnr_on_amd.ini with the mod settings. Uninstalling restores the folder as it was.
A first install starts with the default settings (image filters off); a game with its own profile (The Witcher 3) starts
with that profile instead. Updating over an older version installs the new settings and keeps your own choices (menu
language and options, structure, style, neural passes, FPS limit, image filters, F10 on/off). The last menu line,
"Restore defaults", puts the menu options back to the mod's general defaults (image filters off, Default style), also in a
game with its own profile; menu language, input blocking and F10 on/off stay as they are.

Requirements: AMD Radeon RX 9070 / RX 9070 XT (RDNA 4); DirectX 12 game with FSR 3.1; a CPU with AVX2 (Intel Core
4th generation / AMD Ryzen or newer; AVX-512 is used when present - without AVX2 the face pass stays off).
Tested only on a Radeon RX 9070 XT, in Resident Evil Requiem, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (next-gen, DirectX 12),
Onimusha: Way of the Sword (demo), Cyberpunk 2077, Monster Hunter Wilds, Shadow of the Tomb Raider,
Red Dead Redemption 2, Stellar Blade (demo) and Final Fantasy XVI (demo).
Keep the payload folder next to the installer.
The game library downloads cover art from Steam's public image servers, only for games that have a Steam app id.
Games from other stores get a plain cover; start the installer with --capas-por-nome to look their covers up on Steam by name.

WARNING (AMD Adrenalin):
Before playing, turn off the Adrenalin metrics overlay and recording/instant replay and set performance tuning
to "Default" (no saved overclock); reboot after changing it. With them on the GPU can become very slow and hang
(black screen/TDR) while the game loads.

WHAT'S NEW IN 1.6.0:
- Greater stability.
- New game library in the installer: it finds the installed games on all stores, shows each game with its cover and
  status (tested, not tested yet, not supported, installed version) and installs, updates or removes the mod with one
  click. Games with anti-cheat are marked and cannot be installed (online games may ban mods).
- New image filters in the mod menu (End), applied to the final image before the game HUD. All of them start off
  (except the ones a game's own profile turns on); turning one on opens its settings right below it (Enter opens and
  closes them):
  - CAS sharpening: AMD FidelityFX CAS adaptive sharpening. Brings out fine detail without jagged edges, stronger where
    contrast is low. Setting: strength.
  - LumaSharpen: sharpens brightness only, without touching color, with a limit that prevents halos. Settings: strength
    and limit.
  - Only face outlines: inside faces, CAS and LumaSharpen sharpen only eyes, lashes, brows, lips, nostrils and the face
    edge; smooth skin is left without sharpening.
  - Local contrast (Clarity): brings out the texture of skin, cloth, stone and wood without changing the overall light of
    the scene. Settings: strength, radius (fine texture to large shapes), midtones (spares deep shadows and highlights),
    zone (everywhere or shadows only) and "Clarity off the face": Off, Skin (keeps the face and neck skin, found by its
    color, out of it) or Head (keeps the whole head out - skin, hair, beard and hat - for games where the skin color is
    not found; the default).
  - Levels: sets the black point and the white point to remove the grey veil and give depth to the shadows; the output
    black and white give a faded or softer look.
  - Tone and color (Tonemap): gamma, exposure, saturation, bleach bypass (desaturated film contrast) and defog (removes
    the haze veil), for a cinematic look.
  - Temperature (K): white balance of the whole image, from 4000 K (warmer, orange) to 9000 K (cooler, blue), 6500 K =
    neutral, plus a green/magenta tint. Brightness does not change.
  - Face skin: adjusts only the skin of the face and neck - light, saturation, hue and temperature - without touching
    hair, clothes or the scene.
  - Color by range (HSL): changes the hue, saturation and lightness of each color range (reds, oranges, yellows, greens,
    cyans, blues, purples, magentas) or of all colors, with a smoothness setting and an option to protect skin tones.
  - Ambient occlusion (AO): adds soft contact shadows in corners, crevices and under objects, computed from the game's
    depth after upscaling and before the HUD. Sky, HUD and bright areas are left as they are. Settings: quality (Low,
    Medium, High, Ultra), intensity, radius, thickness (lets light pass behind thin objects, fewer halos), range,
    protect skin, less AO in bright areas, resolution (Automatic, Full, Half) and a debug view. Off by default; large
    radius values can make grass and foliage shimmer.
  - Indirect lighting (IL): light bounced from nearby surfaces (one bounce) - a red wall tints the floor next to it,
    sunlit grass tints what is close to it green. Computed from the game's depth and colors after upscaling and before
    the HUD; sky and HUD are left as they are. Settings: style (Subtle, Default, Strong), intensity, saturation, bounce
    brightness (0 = color only, 100 = full light), quality, update (every frame or in rotation, cheaper), protect skin
    and a debug view. Off by default; if the game already has global illumination or ray tracing, keep the bounce
    brightness low.
- "Restore defaults" line at the end of the menu.
- Shadow of the Tomb Raider support. The game has no FSR, so the installer adds it: in the game settings choose XeSS as
  the upscaler (the mod turns XeSS into FSR 3.1). Frame generation also works here (through XeSS): a "Frame generation"
  line in the mod menu, off at every launch (turning it on lasts for that session only); the image filters, ambient
  occlusion and indirect lighting pause while it is on.
- Red Dead Redemption 2 support (DirectX 12), with frame generation: in the game's graphics settings set the API to
  DirectX 12 and choose AMD FSR 2 - the mod upgrades it to FSR 4. The installer adds the FSR files the game needs.
  A "Frame generation" line appears in the mod menu (End); it starts off every time the game opens. Turned on, it adds
  one generated frame between two game frames (almost twice the FPS on screen, about one frame more latency; the game
  HUD can shimmer while the camera turns), and the compact counter shows "FG x2".
- About frame generation: the mod's own frame generation (x2) is there for games that do not have frame generation
  of their own - Red Dead Redemption 2 (through its FSR 2) and Shadow of the Tomb Raider (through its XeSS). In games
  that already offer frame generation, just turn it on in the game's own graphics menu; the mod works with it.
- Stellar Blade and Final Fantasy XVI support.
- Option to hide the compact FPS counter ("Compact counter" in the menu), for OLED screens.
- Compatibility improvements.
